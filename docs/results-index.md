## 1. How to use this index

This document indexes every result recorded for the project up to commit `03660fb2`: the numbers, the protocol behind each number, the experiment folder that produced it, and the file where it can be checked. It is organised by the project's three lines of work (Line A: the original framework with a pixel renderer; Line B: pixel-free variants; Line C: evaluation assets), followed by a map of all 83 `experiments/*` folders and a list of open gaps. Raw result files written before 2026-11-09 are no longer in the tree but are readable at commit `5d4405bb` (`git show 5d4405bb:<path>`), and all RoboCasa results (2026-11-10 onward) exist only on the EFS under `/efs/prescottsun/SPT-JEPA/runs/`, with their numbers quoted as prose in `experiments/CONCLUSIONS.md`, `HANDOFF.md` and `experiments/robocasa_stable_scaling/PROTOCOL.md`. Every table row ends in a source: a file path (with a line number where the audit recorded one) and, where it applies, the experiment folder.

### 1.1 Notation and terms used in every table

**Source paths.**

- Paths are repository-relative. To keep tables narrow, the leading `experiments/` is dropped: a path that starts with one of the folder names of Section 5, or with `CONCLUSIONS.md` or `best-configs.json`, is under `experiments/`. Paths starting with `as_jepa_lewm/`, `as_jepa_tools/`, `docs/`, `HANDOFF.md` or `AGENTS.md` are at the repository root.
- A path with no prefix is in the tree at `03660fb2`.
- The prefix `H:` marks a file that exists only in history, at commit `5d4405bb`. Example: `H:promising20261029/results/epoch3/DECISION.md` is read with `git show 5d4405bb:experiments/promising20261029/results/epoch3/DECISION.md`.
- `C.md:205` means line 205 of `experiments/CONCLUSIONS.md` at `03660fb2`. `SS/` abbreviates `experiments/robocasa_stable_scaling/`.
- The prefix `EFS:` marks a path under `/efs/prescottsun/SPT-JEPA/`. The EFS was not reachable during the audit, so EFS locations are taken from the notes.
- Documents on Lingjing's branches are cited with their commit, for example `docs/lingjing/decoder-free/handoff-two-tracks.md` at `a6211003`.

**Terms.**

- *Planning success* (called P0 in the repository) is closed-loop success in percent when a planner drives the simulator with the learned model.
- *Primary / fresh* are two fixed sets of held-out start and goal cases, always reported as `primary / fresh`. On PushT they are two sets of planning seeds (42, 43, 44 for primary; 1042, 1043, 1044 for fresh; 50 episodes per seed). On TwoRoom and Cube they are 50 fixed cases each. Fresh cases come from the same validation pool as primary cases and were inspected during research, so they are not an independent test set.
- *Strict* success, used on RoboCasa Reach, means the final distance to the goal is under 2 mm. *Return* on the same task is the mean normalised progress reward of the public Reach environment: a coarse measure, not a success rate.
- *CEM* is the cross-entropy method planner: it samples action sequences, scores them with the model, and refits the sampling distribution to the best ones.
- *Loader epoch* is one pass of the data loader. Its length in updates depends on the window size, so equal epochs do not mean equal updates.
- *DEV* is the 64-case RoboCasa Reach development set that the collaborator's runs evaluate every hour. A separate 32-case confirmation set is sealed and has never been evaluated.
- "Audit arithmetic" and "audit inference" mark values or readings that the audits derived and that the collaborator's notes do not state.

**Attribution.** "Lingjing's specifications" are the method and experiment documents under `docs/lingjing/`. "The collaborator's runs" are everything that was trained and evaluated in this repository.

## 2. Line A: the original framework

The original framework has three parts: an image encoder that gives one noisy state per frame, a predictor that forecasts the next state from past states and actions, and a pixel renderer that redraws each frame from its state and an anchor frame (another frame of the same episode that supplies appearance). The training loss is the renderer's flow-matching loss plus `beta` times a rate: the KL divergence, in nats, between each frame's noisy state and the predictor's forecast of it. There is no EMA target, no pretrained initialisation and no augmentation.

## 3. Line B: pixel-free variants

These variants train no pixel term. Subsections 3.1 to 3.4 come from Lingjing's specifications on branch `lingjing/decoder-free-as-jepa`; subsection 3.5 is the objective that the collaborator's runs arrived at on RoboCasa. ### 3.1 The coding-rate (log-det) objective

**What it is.** The renderer is replaced by a reward computed in state space: the information that a frame's noisy state carries beyond the anchor frame's state, estimated as a Gaussian coding rate (a log-determinant of the covariance of anchor-relative states). Two estimators exist. The pooled one, `0.5 * logdet(I + E[C_i] / sigma^2)`, uses one covariance across all windows of a batch, so it couples samples. The per-window one, `0.5 * E[logdet(I + C_i / sigma^2)]`, is a per-sample loss. The rate and the predictor are kept; there is no decoder, EMA, stop-gradient or augmentation. Repo class: `DecoderFreeLeWM` (`as_jepa_lewm/decoder_free.py`). Specification: `docs/lingjing/decoder-free/spec.md` and `story.md` at `fbb3efa6`. All recipes used independent noise per frame (`rho = 0`); the class has no `rho` argument.

### 3.3 The window InfoNCE control

**What it is.** A contrastive estimate of the same information, with no extra parameters: each noisy state must be identified among a set of candidate frames, scored by its Gaussian log-likelihood under each candidate's encoded mean. The resulting InfoNCE value is a lower bound on the information, capped at the logarithm of the number of candidates. Two versions exist. In the round-2 version (`NCELeWM`, `as_jepa_lewm/nce.py`) the candidates are all rendered frames of the batch, so the loss couples samples across the batch and the project's per-sample rule treats it as a control, not as a candidate method. In the later version (`window_nce`, the `estimator="nce"` option of `as_jepa_lewm/anchor_reader.py`) the candidates are only the frames of the same window plus the anchor.

### 3.5 The current RoboCasa Reach3D mainline

**Objective in plain words.** The encoder is trained so that moving the robot by a commanded displacement moves the code by a fixed linear image of that command, and so that nothing else in the code changes between consecutive frames. Errors are measured relative to the size of the command, and large errors are discounted by a logarithm. For each of the 7 transitions in an 8-frame window, with `E` the encoder, `a` the executed 14-dimensional command and `Q` a learned 128 by 14 matrix with orthonormal columns:

```
e    = E(x_next) - (E(x_now) + Q a)
r    = |e|^2 / (2 * sigma0^2 * |Q a|^2),   sigma0 = 0.1
loss = mean over transitions of 0.5 * log(1 + 2 r)
```

Both encodings receive gradients. There is no pixel term, no anchor frame, no sampled noise, no learned predictor (the transition is the parameter-free map `z + Q a`), no entry KL, no `beta`, no stop-gradient, no EMA and no batch statistic. A renderer module is still constructed but is frozen and never called (`SS/current_entropy.py:351`). Collapse is prevented by the action embedding: `|Q a|` is fixed by the action, so a constant encoder pays `0.5 * log(101)`, about 2.31, on every transition with a non-zero action, while exact transport pays 0. The repository entry is `robocasa_reach3d_scratch_consequence_current_head` (`best-configs.json:7-46`); the loss is `conditional_rate_loss` in `SS/current_entropy.py:206-296`.

**Relation to the original framework.** Kept: an image encoder trained from scratch with one state per time step, no EMA, no pretrained weights, per-sample losses. Dropped: the pixel renderer and anchor, the sampled state noise (`sigma0` survives as a constant), the learned predictor, the entry KL and `beta`, and the correlated-noise and forecast-rendering additions. The pixel term was first removed on 2026-11-17 in the run `consequence-likelihood-only-20261117` (`C.md:205`). The notes record no explicit decision with a stated reason; they record paired experiments in which pixel-free arms kept the coarse control quality of pixel arms with about half the trained parameters (82.36% at 1 h with 22.220M trainable parameters against 77.62% at 2 h for the paired pixel model, with the caution that objective and active parameters both differ), after which all gains in strict success came from pixel-free descendants.

**Architecture, data and budget.** ViT encoder on raw 224 px RGB, patch 14, random initialisation; 22.223504M trainable parameters of 40.863772M in the module (`C.md:217`, `C.md:229`); encoder width 384 is inferred from that count. One 128-dimensional state per time step for two synchronized cameras (8 learned queries of 16 dimensions, `SS/joint_forward.py:23-104`). Data: 12,518 trajectories of 17 frames from two cameras, collected with random commands in 58 training kitchen combinations, plus 512 rotation logs; only RGB and the issued commands are stored (`SS/stream.py:60-71`). Training: 4 GPUs per run, 32 windows per update; the 12-hour parent has 249,539 updates (`best-configs.json:12,20`).

**Evaluation protocol.** As in Section 2.6: frozen body; a head of 384 linear weights on the 128-dimensional code difference, fitted by ridge regression on 17,920 transitions with 1,536 for validation; at most 5 commands; 64 DEV cases; strict success under 2 mm (`best-configs.json:25-44`).

**Stated limits.**

- Every number is on the same 64 development cases, which have been evaluated hundreds of times. The 32 confirmation cases remain sealed (`best-configs.json:45`).
- One task. The linear readout was never run on Push (`robocasa_action_probe/probe.py:361-363` hardcodes the task `"reach"`), and the notes state that a constant additive transition limits contact, clamping and object interaction, so the positive Reach signal cannot stand in for manipulation (`C.md:239`).
- Training hours alone do not scale strict success: the hourly curves rise for 5 to 7 hours and then oscillate.
- The objective contains an inverse-dynamics signal. With orthonormal `Q`, `|d - Q a|^2 = |Q^T d - a|^2 + |(I - Q Q^T) d|^2` for the code change `d`: a regression of the executed action from the code change plus a penalty on change outside the action subspace. The notes write this out and conclude that the structure cannot be called free of an inverse signal (`C.md:239`).
- The result depends on the head's regulariser by several cases, and most failures are in the last visual correction: 25 of 29 final failures of an earlier 35 of 64 model were already within 10 mm before the last command (`HANDOFF.md:33`).
- No comparison with public RoboJEPA under the same protocol exists; the notes say stable precise-control scaling and that comparison remain to be verified (`C.md:3`; `best-configs.json:45`).
- Audit inference, not a statement in the notes: with `predict = z + Q a` and a squared-distance goal cost, the best plan is the straight line `Q^T (g - z)`, so planning with this model adds almost nothing over the readout by construction. A label-free inverse of this kind was tested on an earlier forward-only model and was weaker than the fitted head (36.54 against 31.50 mm validation action error, `C.md:213`).
- The run's configuration file is not in the tree (hash only), so the code path of the mainline is inferred from assertions in the code and the description in the notes.

### 4.3 Planner score and linear readout moved in opposite directions

On the same checkpoints of the 4-slot framework head on frozen V-JEPA 2.1-G, and on the same 8 older cases, the CEM return fell from 78.85% at 1 h to 73.37% at 8 h while the return of the linear readout rose from 81.37% to 85.70% (`C.md:296`; folders `robocasa_time_compare`, `robocasa_action_probe`). The notes draw two cautions from this: the regression under CEM does not by itself show that the representation stopped improving, and the readout adds supervision on the current action and reads only about 472.5 thousand of the head's 45.92M parameters without using the predictor or the renderer, so it cannot replace a measurement of the full world model. The notes contain further cases where the head's action error improved and closed-loop control got worse (`C.md:253`, `C.md:259`), and `AGENTS.md:37` states that a lower prediction or inverse error is not control progress.

### 4.4 Pieces that do not exist yet for the two-stage evaluation protocol

The protocol is in `docs/lingjing/as-jepa-evaluation-handoff.md` (branch `lingjing/anchored-state-jepa`, `484f10f3`). The existing Reach3D evaluation is a frozen-body, closed-loop, linear-readout evaluation on one task; it matches the outer shape of the frozen regime and is not E1.

Project rules in `AGENTS.md` and `SS/PROTOCOL.md` that restrict some of these pieces, recorded here because they determine what can be run in the shared repository:

- The action readout may be supervised only by the executed action of the same transition, the representation must be frozen, and no action-label gradient may enter the world model (`AGENTS.md:30`; `SS/PROTOCOL.md:23,614`). This excludes partial and full fine-tuning with action labels.
- Future or optimal action labels are forbidden (`AGENTS.md:15,29`). A head that outputs a 16-action chunk has 15 future-action targets.
- Demonstration logs may be used as transition records but not to train expert paths, policies or goal-conditioned action labels, and goal images are for planning only (`AGENTS.md:28,31`).
- Learner inputs are RGB history and executed actions only; reward, success, state, task ID and language enter neither the learner nor the planning cost (`AGENTS.md:27`). Collection does not read or store pose or state (`SS/PROTOCOL.md:48,603,634-635`).
- The whole learning chain starts from random initialisation with no external pretrained model (`AGENTS.md:23-24`); frozen V-JEPA 2.1 is an authorised exception for the encoder only and is not reported together with the from-scratch line (`AGENTS.md:25-26`).
- Candidate methods use per-sample losses; only baselines may keep batch regularisers (`AGENTS.md:32-33`).
- Repeated evaluation of the same cases and descendants of one parent do not count as independent cases or seeds; a scaling claim needs several budget points, new confirmation cases, no late regression and repeatable strict success (`AGENTS.md:38`; `SS/PROTOCOL.md:640-641`).
- One point runs the other way: the handoff's stage 1 requires that nothing in the objective predicts actions, while the mainline's objective is, by the algebra in `C.md:239`, an action regression in a learned orthonormal frame plus a penalty on other change.

## 6. Open gaps

Experiments that are missing for a defensible claim, as listed by the audits. The line was closed for the small benchmarks on 2026-11-03 (`lingjing_fullspec_20261102/CLOSEOUT_20261103.md`), so the Line A items are open, not in progress.

### Line A: the original framework

PushT:

1. A same-campaign comparison with the LeWM baseline: TF (or F8) and L0 trained in one campaign with the same seeds, evaluated on verified identical episodes, with a paired interval and the budget matched in a stated unit (updates, frame-forwards or GPU-hours). The existing comparison is 2 against 3 seeds across campaigns.
2. F8 beyond 2 epochs and beyond 2 seeds. The planned continuation (3 epochs for seeds 1 and 2, seeds 3 and 4 to 2 epochs) was not run.
3. A reconciliation of the specification with the factorial results: the canonical cell (`rho = 0`, sampled one-step forecast, `lam = 1`) scores 8%, the working configuration needs `rho = 0.8`, and noise-free means beat priced samples in every pairing.
4. Whether the collapsed cells (F1, F5, F7, F9, the calibrated arms) are slow or dead: they were stopped at 2 epochs, and the original method is known to recover between 2 and 3 epochs.
5. TF and FO inside the factorial class: the (0.5, rollout, mean) cells were not rerun, so every comparison with them is historical.
6. An explanation of the calibration result (calibrated runs are worse than the sham on PushT), and a calibrated run of F8 or TF, which does not exist.
7. A 10-epoch run of a renderer model, since L0 gains 6 to 9 points between 3 and 10 epochs.

TwoRoom:

8. Any learned-encoder model of the framework that passes the state-use gate. None exists.
9. F8 on TwoRoom, and the canonical objective with a spatial, non-pooled state and a learned encoder.
10. A designed pair, at 6,000 updates, of the same frozen observations with and without the renderer objective, to show what the frozen carrier owes to the renderer.

Cube:

11. F8 on Cube, and any round-2 arm beyond 12,000 updates.
12. More seeds and cases: two seeds and 50 cases per set, with 2 to 4 points of replay variation, cannot resolve differences of 4 to 9 points.
13. A model-level result under the standard planner: every Cube gain so far is a controller gain that also lifts the baseline.
14. A change that fixes the goal geometry (latent distance follows the arm, not the cube); no anchor rule or `sigma0` value changed it.

All three small benchmarks:

15. Generation evidence for the claimed models: FVD, LPIPS, SSIM and rollout videos exist only for early PushT models, nothing for TwoRoom or Cube, and the human gallery gate is unreviewed.
16. Capacity and compute matching: 51.1M parameters plus renderer compute against about 18.0M, with no baseline given the extra capacity or compute.
17. Baseline fidelity: the native baselines are adaptations (window 8 and history 7 on TwoRoom and Cube, window 4 with the project's split on PushT, 3 to 10 epochs against an official default of 100); released LeWM scores 72 / 68 and 30 / 24 on these custom cases.
18. An untouched test set and training-seed uncertainty: all cases come from the validation pool and were inspected during research, and all intervals condition on the trained models.
19. One recipe across the three tasks: the three best results use different models, controllers and budgets.

Meta-World, DROID and RoboCasa:

20. Any manipulation success of the framework on Meta-World (0% on six tasks), and an independent confirmation of the reaching result (2 parents, same 20 inspected cases).
21. A reproduction of DINO-WM or JEPA-WMs on Meta-World under a matched protocol (never started; official data gated).

23. A from-scratch checkpoint of the framework with its renderer at scale on RoboCasa Reach3D, with more than one seed, and any strict success above 4 of 64 with a pixel term.
